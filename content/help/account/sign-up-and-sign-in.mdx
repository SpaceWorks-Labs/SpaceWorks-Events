You need an account to run events, keep your registrations in one place, or see your member ID cards. You **don't** need one to register for most events: guests can sign up with just a name and email.

## Create an account with email

<Steps>

1. Choose **Sign in** in the site header and open the **Create account** tab.
2. Enter your **Name**, **Email** and a **Password**, then choose **Create account**.
3. Open the verification email and follow its link.
4. On the **Confirm your email** page, choose **Confirm email**. You're taken back to the sign-in page with "Email verified. You can now sign in."

</Steps>

<Screenshot name="login-page" alt="The sign-in page with the Sign in and Create account tabs" />

The page always answers "If this email can be registered, check your inbox for a verification link", so nobody can use the form to find out who has an account. If you already have an account with that address, no new one is made; sign in or [reset your password](/help/account/forgotten-password) instead. Sign-ups are also limited to a few an hour for each address and network; past that the page gives the same answer but sends nothing, so if no email arrives, wait an hour and try again.

### Password rules

- 12 to 128 characters. Long passwords with accented letters or non-Latin scripts can hit a lower limit; if so, the app asks you to use fewer characters.
- Not a password found in a known data breach. The app checks a public list of breached passwords without ever sending your password, and tells you if yours "has appeared in a data breach".

The same rules apply when you reset or change your password.

<Callout kind="note">

The verification link works once and expires after one hour. If it has expired, sign in with your email and password: the app asks you to verify first and shows **Resend verification email**.

</Callout>

## Sign in

<Steps>

1. Open the sign-in page and stay on the **Sign in** tab.
2. Enter your **Email** and **Password** and choose **Sign in**.
3. If you use [two-step verification](/help/account/two-step-verification), enter the code from your authenticator app on the **Verify it's you** screen and choose **Verify and sign in**.
4. If the app asks you to **Check your email**, enter the six-digit code it emailed you. See [Codes for new devices](/help/account/sign-up-and-sign-in#codes-for-new-devices).

</Steps>

If you haven't confirmed your email yet, the app says so and shows **Resend verification email**. Forgotten your password? Choose **Forgot password?** above the password field.

## Codes for new devices

The app remembers the browsers you've signed in from. When you sign in with your password from a browser it doesn't recognise, it emails you a six-digit code and shows **Check your email**. Enter the code from that email in **Code from the email** and choose **Verify and sign in**.

- The code expires after 10 minutes and works once. Starting the sign-in again sends a new code, and the earlier one stops working. A wrong or old code shows "That code isn't right, or it has expired."
- Your very first sign-in doesn't need a code.
- If you use an authenticator app, its code is proof enough, so you're never asked for an emailed code as well.
- Clearing a browser's site data, or a private window, makes it look new again.

<Callout kind="warning">

If you get a sign-in code you didn't ask for, someone knows your password. Reset it from the sign-in page straight away, and never share the code with anyone.

</Callout>

## Sign-in protection

To stop people guessing passwords, the app counts unsuccessful sign-ins:

- After several wrong passwords on your account in a short time, even the right password needs an emailed code.
- After many more, sign-in is paused for a while, codes included.
- A network that keeps failing is blocked for up to an hour. You see "Too many unsuccessful sign-ins. Wait an hour and try again, or reset your password."

Only five sign-in codes can be emailed to an account each hour.

## New sign-in alerts

When someone signs in to your account with its password from a new browser or device, you get a "New sign-in to your account" email naming the browser and system. If it was you, there's nothing to do. If it wasn't, reset your password and use **Sign out everywhere**; see [Profile, appearance and security](/help/account/profile-and-security#sign-out-everywhere). New devices also appear in your **Recent security activity**. A sign-in that doesn't identify its device, such as one made by a script, counts as a new device every time.

## Continue with Google

If the person who runs this server has set up Google sign-in, the sign-in page shows **Continue with Google**. Otherwise it says "Google sign-in is not configured for this instance."

- **New here?** Continuing with Google creates your account using your Google name and verified email. There's no separate verification email.
- **Already have an account with the same email?** Google is linked to it, so you can sign in either way. If your account's email isn't verified yet, confirm it first, or the Google sign-in is refused.

With Google, Google's own security protects the sign-in, and there are no emailed device codes. Organisation staff still confirm an authenticator code before their dashboards open. You can add a password later from **Account settings** → **Security**; see [Profile, appearance and security](/help/account/profile-and-security#password).

## Good to know

- One account can belong to several organisations.
- The person who runs this server can turn off new accounts. The **Create account** tab then disappears, and Google sign-in for a new address shows "New account creation is currently unavailable." Existing accounts can still sign in.
- Registrations you make while signed in appear under **Account settings** → **Registrations**. Registrations made without signing in stay reachable from their emails but aren't added to your account later.
- To sign out, open your account menu (your name or picture in the sidebar) and choose **Sign out**.
- Emails not arriving? See [Common problems](/help/troubleshooting/common-problems).
