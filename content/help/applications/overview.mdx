Sometimes you have more interest than places, or you want to choose who comes: a workshop with 30 seats and 200 hopefuls, a conference that reviews speaker applications, a fellowship, or a hackathon that picks its teams. Applications turn registration into an application. People apply with your registration form, your reviewers score them in one or more stages, and you offer places. A place is confirmed only when the applicant accepts it, and pays for it if the ticket costs money.

Applications work on any one-off event: free or paid, people registering on their own or as teams. Recurring series can't take applications, because their registrations are per date.

<Callout kind="permission">

Turning applications on or off and changing their settings: owners and admins of the organisation. Stages, reviewers, decisions and offers: owners and admins, and co-organisers with **Manage registrations**. Reviewing: anyone with the **Review applications** permission, and owners and admins.

</Callout>

## Turning applications on

<Steps>

1. Open the event and choose the **Settings** tab.
2. In **Applications and shortlisting**, choose **Take applications**.
3. A new **Applications** tab appears. Its first stage, **Review**, is open and ready.

</Steps>

You can turn applications on or off only before anyone has registered, so every registration is an application or none is. Crew don't count: they never apply. While applications are on, the event stays **approval-only**; the **Privacy** setting can't change until you turn applications off.

## An example: a free workshop

Say you run a free, hands-on workshop with 30 seats.

<Steps>

1. Set the event **Capacity** to 30 and add a question or two to the registration form, such as "What do you hope to build?".
2. Turn on applications. Everyone who registers is now an applicant, shown as **In review**.
3. Invite two volunteers as co-organisers with **Review applications**. In **Applications** → **Reviewers**, assign each application to both.
4. When the reviews are in, select the strongest 30 in **Review and decide**, choose **Offer a place**, and **Release** the batch.
5. Each person gets an email with a link to their ticket page, where they choose **Accept**. Their place is confirmed straight away and they get their ticket.
6. Put a few more on the waitlist. If someone declines or doesn't answer in time, the next person on the waitlist is offered the place automatically.

</Steps>

Nothing in this flow involves money, orders or payment holds.

## Free and paid places

- **Free places** are confirmed as soon as the applicant accepts the offer, with the usual ticket email.
- **Paid places** are never confirmed unpaid. The applicant chooses **Accept and pay**: their place is held for a day while they pay through the payment link, and the payment confirms it. If they don't pay in time, the offer stands again until its answer-by time, then expires.
- A **free ticket type on a paid event** (a scholarship ticket, say) works like a free place for whoever chose it.
- On **team events** the team applies as one, and the team leader answers the offer. When places cost money, the leader pays once for the whole team: set who pays to **The team leader** in **Settings** before turning applications on. Paying per member isn't supported with applications yet.

## Settings

In **Applications** → **Overview**:

- **Decisions**: **Reviewed** (you release decisions in batches) or **First come, first served** (each application is offered a place as it arrives, until the offers run out, then the waitlist).
- **Days to accept an offer**: 1 to 30. An offer never runs past the start of the event.
- **Most offers out at once**: offers that are open or accepted. Leave it empty to use the event capacity.
- **Email applicants when they move to the next stage**: off by default. Offers, reminders, waitlist places, expired offers and "not successful" decisions are always emailed, because they carry a decision or a deadline.

## Good to know

- The ordinary **Approve**, **Decline** and **Promote** buttons in **Guests** don't work for applications: decide them in the **Applications** tab, where reviews and offers are checked.
- Hackathons can use applications to choose their teams, then judge projects as usual in the **Judging** tab.
