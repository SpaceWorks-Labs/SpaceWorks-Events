Some events pay back participants' costs, such as travel to a hackathon. When the organisers allow it, you ask for the money from your ticket page: say what it was for, how much it cost, and add photos of your receipts. The organisers decide each claim and email you.

## Who can claim

Your place must be **confirmed** (not waitlisted, waiting for approval or declined). Then you can claim when either:

- the organisers have turned on expense claims for participants on this event, or
- a **reimbursement policy** is open to you. Organisers set these up by stage, for example travel for the finalists of a judging round, for teams that were mentored in a round, or for crew. Some only open once finalists are published, only during a set window, or only for people who were checked in at the event.

If so, your ticket page has a **Claim expenses** section. If you don't see it, nothing is open to you right now: ask the organisers. A policy can appear later, for example when you're announced as a finalist. If the organisers turned on the email, you're told once when a policy opens to you.

If you registered while signed in, sign in to that account to claim. Recurring series don't take expense claims.

## Send a claim

<Steps>

1. Open your ticket. See [Your ticket](/help/attending/your-ticket) if you can't find it.
2. Under **Claim expenses**, if you see **Claim under**, choose the reimbursement policy (or **General expenses**). It shows how much is left for you.
3. Choose what it was for in **What for**. Under a policy, only the categories it covers are listed.
4. Describe it in **Details**: where you went or what you bought, and why (up to 1,000 characters).
5. Enter the **Amount** in the event's currency, for example 1200 or 1200.50.
6. Add up to three **Receipt photos**.
7. Choose **Send claim**.

</Steps>

### Categories

**What for** has these choices: Travel, Accommodation, Food, Venue, Equipment, Printing, Marketing, Supplies and Other.

### Receipt photos

Add photos of your tickets, bills or receipts so the organisers can check the amount. You can add up to three images (JPEG, PNG or WebP), under 5 MB in all. Photos are cleaned of hidden information, such as where they were taken, and stored privately. Only the organisers see them.

## The cap per person

The organisers can set the most they pay back to each person. If they have, the section says so ("up to … per person").

The general cap covers all your claims without a policy together. Claims that are waiting count at the amount you asked for; decided ones count at the amount approved. Rejected and withdrawn claims don't count. If a new claim would go over, the form tells you how much is left ("You can claim up to … in all; … is left.").

### Reimbursement policies

Each policy open to you is listed above the form with its name, the most it pays each person, how much is left for you and what it covers. What's left is the smaller of your own remaining cap and the policy's remaining budget, so it can go down when others claim too. Claims under one policy don't use up another policy's cap or the general cap.

Your claims show which policy they were sent under. When the organisers decide a claim, they check you still qualify: if, for example, your check-in was undone or your project was withdrawn, they can't approve it.

## What happens next

Each claim in the list shows its amount, category and status:

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Waiting** | The organisers haven't decided yet. You can still withdraw it. |
| **Approved** | They'll pay it back. They may approve less than you asked, shown as "… of …". |
| **Paid** | They've paid you back. |
| **Rejected** | They won't pay it. Their reason is shown under the claim. |
| **Withdrawn** | You took it back. |

You're emailed when a claim is approved, rejected or paid. Any note from the organisers appears under the claim.

The organisers pay you themselves, for example by bank transfer, UPI or cash, and then mark the claim as paid.

## Withdraw a claim

Changed your mind, or made a mistake? While a claim is **Waiting**, choose **Withdraw** next to it. You see "Claim withdrawn." To correct a claim, withdraw it and send a new one.

Once the organisers have decided, you can't withdraw it. Contact them instead.

## Good to know

- Your ticket link is what proves the claim is yours, so keep it private.
- A claim is only for your own costs. Organisers file claims for speakers and volunteers themselves.
- Organisers: see [Expense claims and reimbursements](/help/finance/expense-claims).

If your ticket changes hands, your claims stay in the organisers? financial records with your original details. The new holder cannot see your claims or decision notes. A claim started with your old ticket link cannot be submitted after the holder changes, even if receipt uploads were already in progress.
