Tickets let you offer more than one way to register: free and paid options, an early-bird price, a group ticket, or a hidden ticket for speakers. Extras are things guests add to their order, like a T-shirt or lunch. An event without tickets keeps the usual free registration.

<Callout kind="permission">

Owners, admins, and co-organisers with the **Edit details** permission on the event or series. Paid tickets also need paid events turned on and a connected payment account: see [Turning on paid events](/help/payments/enable-paid-events).

</Callout>

## Adding a ticket

<Steps>

1. Open the event and choose the **Settings** tab. For a recurring series, open the series page instead.
2. In the **Tickets** card, choose **Add ticket**.
3. Enter a **Ticket name**, such as "General admission", and an optional **Description**.
4. Choose **Fixed price** or **Pay what you want**, and enter the price. Leave the price blank or 0 for a free ticket.
5. Set any limits, the sales window and **Who can buy it**, as described below.
6. Choose **Add ticket**.

</Steps>

<Screenshot name="event-settings-tickets" alt="The Tickets card on an event's Settings tab with a free and a paid ticket, and the form for adding another" />

The list shows each ticket's price, how many are confirmed or being paid for, and its limit. An event can have up to 20 tickets.

## Prices and fees

- **Fixed price** charges the amount you enter, in the event's currency. Buyers see it exactly as entered, so include any tax in it.
- **Pay what you want** lets buyers choose the amount. The **Minimum** is the least they may pay (blank or 0 lets them pay nothing), **Suggested** is the starting amount, and **Most a buyer may pay** caps it. Good for donations and sliding scales.
- **Booking fee per ticket** is added on top of the price and paid by the buyer. It's shown before they pay, and left off whenever those tickets cost nothing: a free price, a pay-what-you-want amount of zero, or a promo code that makes them free.

Each provider has a smallest price it accepts; if yours is below it, the app tells you the minimum when you save. Above ₹1 lakh, the form warns that buyers may need a card or netbanking, as UPI generally stops there.

Editing a ticket's price only changes it for new orders. People who already registered keep the price they were quoted.

## Limits and sales windows

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Limit** | How many of this ticket can be sold. Blank: only the event's capacity applies. The event's capacity always stays the overall limit. |
| **Fewest per order** and **Most per order** | How many of this ticket one order may include, from 1 to 100. |
| **Sales start** and **Sales end** | When the ticket can be bought, in your device's time zone. Leave either blank for no limit. |

At checkout, the places are held for 30 minutes while the buyer pays. If the hold runs out unpaid, the places go back on sale and nothing is charged.

## Group tickets

Set **People per ticket** above 1, up to 20, for a group ticket such as a family ticket. Each person gets their own ticket to check in; the buyer adds their names and emails on the order page. A group ticket counts as one ticket against its **Limit**, but every person counts against the event's capacity. Group tickets aren't available on team events, where each person registers for themselves.

## Hidden and members-only tickets

**Who can buy it** has three choices:

- **Everyone**: anyone who can see the event.
- **Only with an access code**: hidden until a buyer enters the code. See [Promo and access codes](/help/payments/promo-and-access-codes).
- **Only organisation members**: shown only to people signed in as members of your organisation.

## Paying at the desk

Tick **Guests may pay at the desk** to let buyers reserve now and pay in person. Their places are held until you cancel them, and you record the money from the guest list when they pay: see [Orders and receipts](/help/payments/orders-and-receipts).

## Taking a ticket off sale

Choose **Take off sale** and confirm. People can no longer choose it, and anyone who already has it keeps their registration. Tickets are never deleted.

## Extras

The **Extras** card, below the tickets, sells things guests can add to their order: a T-shirt, lunch, a workshop place or a donation. Extras don't take a place at the event, and promo codes never discount them.

<Steps>

1. In the **Extras** card, choose **Add extra**.
2. Enter a **Name**, an optional **Description** and a **Price** (blank or 0 for free).
3. Optionally set **How many in all** and **Most per order**.
4. Choose **Add extra**.

</Steps>

To stop selling one, choose **Take off sale**. Orders that include it keep it. A custom check-in list can admit only the people who bought an extra, such as a lunch queue: see [Custom check-in lists](/help/check-in/check-in-lists).

## Ticket transfers

In the **Ticket transfers** card, tick **Allow transfers** to let buyers give a ticket to someone else from their order page, until **Until this many hours before the start**. The new holder is emailed a new ticket link, and the old one stops working.

## Good to know

- On an event that needs approval, buyers of paid tickets sign up first and pay after you approve them, through an emailed payment link that holds their places for 24 hours. The same happens when a place frees up for someone on the waitlist. They get one reminder a few hours before the link runs out.
- The **Refund policy** card appears once a ticket has a price. See [Refunds and cancellations](/help/payments/refunds).
- For team events see [Paid team places](/help/payments/paid-team-places); for series passes see [Passes](/help/payments/passes).
